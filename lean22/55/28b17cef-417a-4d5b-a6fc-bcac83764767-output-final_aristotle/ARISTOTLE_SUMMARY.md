# Summary of changes for run cb0fb194-1ae7-4d1f-958b-71e9703c1f4a
I worked out what the spreadsheet table means and then verified every cell of it against the machine in Lean. Nothing is left unproved in the files (no `sorry`), and `lake build` is clean.

**How the table reads.** The tape is cut into tokens: two-cell *digit blocks* `h l` with a high digit `h ∈ {0,2}` and a low digit `l ∈ {0,1,2,3}` (the columns `22 21 20 23 02 00 01`), plus cells holding `1`, `3` or `4`, which cannot be a high digit and so sit *loose* and shift the alignment of everything to their right (the columns `1`, `4`, `223`, `023`). Row `A` is the head itself arriving at a token from the left in state `A` — the only signal the machine ever sends across a token boundary, since every passage also ends in state `A`. A cell gives the token's *settled* content, i.e. what it holds once the head has finally left it to the left, together with the crossings of the token's right edge that the arrival causes (`22 A A` = crosses right, returns, crosses right again, returns, then passes on left). Rows `2>` and `0>` are not head signals at all: they are the re-alignment bookkeeping, `d> h l ⇒ (d h) l>`, which re-cuts the word after a loose cell has been consumed and changes no tape cell.

**Result: the table is correct.** All eleven cells of row `A` are exactly right, settled values included (`RequestProject/TableEntries.lean`), resting on seventeen local passage lemmas proved by direct computation over an arbitrary tape (`RequestProject/Transits.lean`), so they hold in any context. The twenty non-halting cells of the shift rows are correct re-cuttings, and the two `---` cells are right: once a shift forms a block `2 4` or `0 4`, the next visit of the head halts the machine — so halting happens exactly when a loose `4` is absorbed into a block. The transition table itself matches the description string `1RB2LA0RB4LB0LA_1LA3LA1RA4RA---` instruction by instruction (`RequestProject/TuringMachine.lean`). Finally, an end-to-end check: running the table as an abstract system reproduces, cell for cell, the machine's actual tape at each of its first seven "epoch boundaries" — the moments at steps 3, 8, 31, 148, 2845, 9082 and 2478517 when the head stands on a fresh blank cell with only blanks to its left (`RequestProject/EpochCheck.lean`, verified by evaluation; the last tape is 106 cells).

The shift-overflow mixed-radix counter structure is visible in the verified rules: the leading run of `22` blocks is a binary counter (each `22` doubles the traversals, `A · 22 = 22 A A`); the remaining low digits cycle `0 → 3 → 1 → 0` with a carry except at `l = 0`; and each epoch adds one cell on the left, shifting the cut of everything to its right, which is what feeds digits into the binary part.

`ANALYSIS.md` documents all of this, including two caveats. The table has no column for a block `03`, nor for a loose `3` after a block other than `22`/`02`; these never arose in the epochs examined, but the table does not prove they cannot. And non-halting is **not** proved — what is proved is that the table's rules are exactly right and reproduce the machine, which reduces the halting question to: can a loose `4` ever be absorbed into a block? The document records the mechanism that appears to prevent it (a loose `4` is always created just right of a `3`, a shift always stops at a `3`, and the signal that degrades that `3` consumes the `4` on the same visit), flagged as an observation rather than a proof. For the record, the machine ran 3·10⁹ steps without halting; that is evidence, not verification.
